# Writing style

Default writing rules for CVs and cover letters. They work well as a starting point; edit them freely.
Claude adds to this file when you give feedback on drafts or run /sync after editing a draft.

## Cover letters: do
- Short and in English unless told otherwise. About one page; usually 5 paragraphs is enough.
- **Every detail must earn its place:** open by naming the posting's core needs, then give each need one paragraph with one piece of evidence and an explicit link back. Cut technical details the reader doesn't need to see the link. Information without a crystal-clear connection to the posting weakens the letter.
- **Coherent, not segmented:** the letter should read as one argument, not a list. Give it a thread and open each paragraph with a transition from the previous one, not with a new "The announcement lists…" every time (use that construction at most once or twice).
- **Loop back:** whenever a paragraph moves outside the posting's domain, its last sentence must tie it back to why it matters for this role.
- One paragraph per experience covering several related requirements, not one paragraph per requirement. Fewer, stronger examples beat many small ones.
- Prefer recent, professional examples over hobby projects when both fit.
- Open with who you are and the exact position: "I am a <year> <degree> student in <field> at <university>, and I am applying for …"
- Refer to "the job announcement" / "the announcement", never to the reader as "you".
- Be concrete: one specific story per paragraph, with a believable detail.
- Explain the technical in plain words a recruiter understands.
- Integrate experience in flowing prose. **No bullet points in letters.**
- Keep sentences short; split long ones.
- Tone: student professional. Natural and flowing, not stiff, not casual.
- If the company has a slogan, work it in naturally (usually in the closing paragraph). Don't recite value lists.
- Close with languages, then "I would very much welcome the opportunity to … at <company> this coming summer."
- Sign off "Kind regards," + "{{NAME}}".
- Layout: plain sender block at the top left (name, city, email, phone); subject line like "Application for Summer Internship <year> in <department>".

## Punctuation
- **Never use em-dashes (—), and avoid semicolons unless absolutely necessary.** Both give the text an AI look. Split into two sentences or use a comma instead.

## Cover letters: avoid
- Describing the company back to them, or claiming "I have been following" the company.
- Addressing "advantage" requirements you don't meet ("my SQL experience is limited, but…"). Leave them out.
- Side examples that dilute a paragraph.
- Generic openers: "I am writing to express my interest in…", "I am excited to apply…", "When I first came across…"
- Unsupported self-praise: "strong foundation", "I am confident that I can contribute meaningfully", "proactive and curious mindset".
- Company flattery that fits any company: "particularly inspired by X's commitment to innovation and sustainability…"
- Reusing the same paragraph across companies.
- Vague claims without a specific: "I have worked with sensors, measurement systems, and testing".
- Typos: always spell-check.

## CVs
- One page, LaTeX. Key Highlights section at the top; all skills grouped in one Skills block.
- One line per bullet where possible; keep hardware model numbers and minor details for interviews.
- Technically specific rather than falsely quantified: never invent a metric to make a bullet look stronger.

## Skills the posting asks for
- **Touched it** (a recorded fact shows some real exposure): list it on the CV with "(basic)", e.g. `SQL (basic)`. Never present it as project experience until a project exists.
- **Never touched it:** don't list it.
- **Never touched it, but the posting clearly wants it** (a requirement or one of a few highlighted tools): /apply asks whether you want to do a small project before sending. If you do, the project is recorded as a fact and the skill is listed.
- Don't spend letter space apologising for missing skills.

## Wording preferences
- Use "built", "designed", "traced", "tested", "evaluated". Avoid "spearheaded", "leveraged", "passionate".
- Use numbers only when they're verified.

## Working with you
- For each new posting: tailor to the position and ask the questions needed to improve it before finalising.
